Overview and objective

This was our undergraduate senior design project in 2022, completed as a team with Kemal Tan Karademir. The report describes an in-silico comparison of selected compounds and protein structures using molecular docking. Although the report title refers to comparing drug effects, the documented work is docking and visualization; it does not measure clinical or biological treatment effects.

Methodology

The report describes selecting ten compounds and two serotonin-transporter structures, 5I6X and 7VOE. Protein structures were obtained from the Protein Data Bank and compounds from PubChem. The workflow prepared PDBQT files, placed docking grid boxes around previously bound ligands, ran AutoDock Vina, and visualized poses in PyMOL.

Results and interpretation

The report discusses docking scores and identifies candidate poses for further comparison. Those scores are computational outputs from a docking workflow. They do not establish measured binding affinity, antidepressant effectiveness, safety, or a treatment recommendation, so no ranking or numeric result is presented here.

Limitations

The report's methods and discussion contain inconsistencies that need review before its numerical scores can be interpreted, including differences in the described compound groups and protein context. Docking alone cannot support clinical claims. This page therefore describes the project process without claiming a therapeutic outcome.

What I learned

Computational screening can help frame structural questions, but its scores need careful validation and cannot substitute for experimental, biological, or clinical evidence.
